The Circle · Member Flow

From expression of interest to engaged member.

One flow for the website developer and the Mighty Networks admin to build from. A member registers interest on www.breakinghundred.org, pays there through Stripe (£299 for 12 months, renewing automatically each year), is invited into Mighty Networks and is added to her spaces automatically. Every step reads and writes one member record, and eighteen automated emails carry her from the first thank you through to renewal.

The payment link goes out on 21 November. Stages 1 to 6 below, from the interest form to the member landing in her spaces, have to be built and tested by 14 November, which leaves a week to fix anything before the link is sent. Stripe is the only payment gateway. Stages 7 to 9 (engagement, giving back, renewal) can follow in December and after.

Who builds it: Website Stripe Mighty Zapier Email Funmi / team

Systems and how they connect

Six systems, with the website database as the single source of truth for every member. No automation should decide anything from Mighty Networks or email data alone. Each step reads and writes the member record. Page paths are suggestions for the developer to adjust.

LayerSystemWhat it does in this flow
Front doorWebsite, www.breakinghundred.orgHosts the interest form, the personal payment page and the contributor form. Creates and updates the member record. Creates Stripe Checkout Sessions.
PaymentsStripe Checkout and Billing, the single gateway for every countryCollects the £299 membership fee in GBP by card as an annual subscription, takes each yearly renewal, and tells the website about every payment, failure and cancellation by webhook.
Member databaseWebsite backend, or an Airtable base if the developer prefersHolds one record per member with the fields and status values below.
EmailA transactional email service sending from the Breaking Hundred domain (for example Brevo, Postmark or Mailchimp Transactional)Sends every email in the catalogue from stored templates with merge fields, and writes each send to email_log.
Automation bridgeZapier, paid plan (multi-step Zaps are needed)Connects the database and email service to Mighty Networks. Runs Z1 and Z2.
PlatformMighty NetworksOne network. Start Here is open to every network member. Academy, Library, Community, Monthly Live and Partner Directory are private spaces added by automation.

What Mighty Networks can do through Zapier

  • Triggers: a member joining, leaving or requesting to join the network or a space, and Course Progress Update, which reports completion percentage.
  • Actions: Invite Members, Add to Space(s) for existing full members, Remove Member (from the network or a space, for members who are not on a plan), Manage Tags, Manage Badges.
  • Delay: Course Progress Update can arrive up to an hour after the member's real progress.
  • No checklist trigger: Mighty Networks does not report space checklist completion to Zapier, so checklist prompts here are time based and use native in-app Automations.

Admin alerts

  • A1: a Mighty Networks sign-up cannot be matched to a member by email. Team inbox, with name and email. Link by hand. No spaces are added.
  • A2: a Stripe payment arrives with a client_reference_id that matches no member. Team inbox. Nothing is sent to the payer automatically.
  • A3: a contributor form is submitted. Community Moderator, plus Academy Director for the content tracks.
  • A4: a membership has ended and Z3 could not remove her access, for example because the Mighty Networks email did not match. Team inbox, so access can be removed by hand.
  • A named person owns this inbox and checks it daily.

Capabilities above come from the Oakvale Summer Intensive developer guide (31 October 2026). The Remove Member action was checked against the Mighty Networks help centre on the same day. The developer should confirm all of them against the current documentation before building.

The member record

Every automation reads one field, status, and moves the member forward one step at a time. Create these fields before building any Zap or email template.

Status values, in order

eoi_registered→ payment_open→ paid→ invited→ joined_network→ in_circle→ academy_complete

When a membership ends, status becomes lapsed. Subscription state, contributor choice, mentor matching and follow up sit in their own fields, so they never replace a status.

Webhook and automation rules

Stripe

  • Verify the signature. Check the Stripe-Signature header with the endpoint secret before reading anything in the request.
  • Act on one event only. Listen for checkout.session.completed and act only when payment_status is paid.
  • Pass the member. Set client_reference_id to the member_id when creating the session, and prefill the customer email.
  • Store verified values. Write amount, currency, reference and time from Stripe's data, never from the browser.
  • Subscription events. Also listen for invoice.paid, invoice.payment_failed, customer.subscription.updated and customer.subscription.deleted, as set out in Stage 9. Each handler follows the same signature and idempotency rules.

Every handler

  • Idempotent on the payment reference. A repeated webhook must never send a second C04 or a second invitation.
  • One price. Stripe has one Price for now, £299, and every email quotes it. If a different Price is introduced later, change the emails and the checkout together.
  • Caps. Every reminder has a maximum, recorded in reminders_sent. When it runs out, set follow_up_flag.
  • Consent. The interest form records agreement to receive Circle emails, and each email gives a way to reply.

Consumer rules to check before launch

Members who pay for themselves are consumers, so UK consumer law applies. This is a summary of what I found, not legal advice. Have the final wording checked by a solicitor or Trading Standards before payments open.

Cancelling within 14 days

  • For a service bought online, the right to cancel starts when the contract is made and lasts 14 days (Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013).
  • If she asks for access to start straight away and then cancels, she can be charged a proportionate amount for what she used. So the checkout needs an express request for immediate access, and C04 says what she gets back.
  • The refund line in C04 is a suggestion built on this. Confirm it, and the wording at checkout, before launch.
  • Outside those 14 days she keeps access until her renewal date, and C04 and C17 both say so.

Automatic renewal

  • The DMCC Act subscription rules were not yet in force in the sources I found. One law firm article (November 2025) pointed to autumn 2026 and called it unconfirmed, and another guide said January 2027. Check the current date. Her first renewal falls in December 2027, so build for these rules now.
  • At sign up: show the price, the annual renewal, the renewal date and how to cancel next to the pay button, with an express tick to agree, not only in the terms.
  • Before renewal: a written reminder a reasonable time ahead. C15 at 30 and 7 days does this.
  • After renewal: a new 14 day cooling off period applies after a renewal onto 12 months or more, with a notice on day one. This is C18, and its wording depends on regulations still to be published.
  • Cancelling: she must be able to cancel online, in the same place she joined, without hidden steps. The Stripe customer portal does this. Do not make email the only route.

Sources: Which?, Consumer Contracts Regulations; TLT, DMCC Act subscription contracts rules; Limio, DMCC Act subscription rules guide. Members based outside the UK may have rights under their own country's rules, so take advice if you expect many.
